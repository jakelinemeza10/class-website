On Photography by Susan Sontag

To me, the meaning behind capturing images and creating graphics are both similar but also differs in ways. Through photography I am able to capture memories from everyday occurrences to special events and trips. While through drawing I am able to build each sketch based on the emotion I feel or use it as a way of distraction. Both photography and creating graphics I am able to gain personal fulfillment as they both allow me to be able to translate how I view the world around me to others. When reading Susan Sontag's opening chapter, "In Plato's Cave," from On Photography, readers read how photography is impacting society in many ways rather than just being something that people use as a form of expression. For example, the text states, “Photographs are perhaps the most mysterious of all the objects that make up, and thicken, the environment we recognize as modern” (Sontag, 3) Here Sontag states how photography can manipulate how people view the world, similar to the story of Plato’s Cave as both show how people's perception of the world can be controlled by the factor of people accepting something rather than wanting to further seek what the truth is.

When I take pictures, I usually tend to lean on capturing things I find pretty or moments where I feel content and know that I would like to keep in order to remember. In the reading, it states, “...photographs alter and enlarge our notions of what is worth looking at and what we have the right to observe” (Sontag, 3) The way that I interpreted this was that each person chooses what they deem as “worthy” to capture as each individual has their own opinion of how much something is valued. Using the last sentence “what we have the right to observe” I think about how a person's choice in what they choose to keep in frame can help define the connection of an individual with the world around them.

At the same time though photographs help us connect to each other. “All photographs are memento mori. To take a photograph is to participate in another person’s (or thing’s) mortality, vulnerability, mutability.” (Sontag, 15) We see how Sontag acknowledges that there are positives in images as it can help society connect through the emotions that a image can produce.

However, there are times that I believe that currently in the world through photography and illustrations people are primarily focused on aesthetics. Now many people are not only addicted to their phones but they are specifically addicted to social media, checking how much likes their post got or watching others and how they live a “perfect” aesthetic life. “Strictly speaking, one never understands anything from a photograph” (Sontag, 23) Here we see how as much as a person seems to connect with an image, there is still a gap in which a person will be unable to reach in order to truly comprehend an image. Furthermore, “Needing to have reality confirmed and experience enhanced by photographs is an aesthetic consumerism to which everyone is now addicted” (Sontag, 24) Sontag continues her argument how many photographs can be meaningless as they can only be used as a way to please others.
